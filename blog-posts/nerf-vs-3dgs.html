NeRF vs 3DGS: a unified view

Jan 15, 2026 9 min read Chen Yu
3DGS NeRF math

Both NeRF and 3DGS aim to represent a 3D scene for novel view synthesis. But their mathematical foundations differ: NeRF uses volumetric ray marching, while 3D Gaussian Splatting projects Gaussians onto the image plane. Interestingly, they can be seen as two sides of the same coin: an integral over 3D primitives.

The NeRF volume rendering equation is:

\[ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \, \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \, \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) \, dt \]

For 3DGS, the accumulated color for a pixel is approximated by blending sorted Gaussians:

\[ C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbf{c}_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) \]

In the limit of infinitesimal Gaussians, the two converge. To illustrate, I recorded a short video comparing both methods on the same scene:

What I find exciting is that future work might unify them in a single differentiable framework. Our recent DeepGfM (NeurIPS 2025) takes a step by using a geometric foundation model that initializes both representations.
