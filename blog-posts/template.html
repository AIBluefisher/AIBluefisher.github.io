Scaling 3D data with Gaussian consensus

Feb 28, 2026 6 min read Chen Yu
3DGS distributed gif

In our recent NeurIPS 2024 work DOGS, we introduced a distributed consensus for 3D Gaussian splatting. One of the key motivations is scaling 3D reconstruction to city-scale scenes while maintaining high quality. Below is a teaser of the pipeline:

The core idea is to treat each Gaussian as a message-passing entity. The update rule can be written as:

\[ \mathbf{G}_i^{(t+1)} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}(i)|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \mathbf{G}_j^{(t)} - \eta \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\mathbf{G}_i^{(t)}) \]

where \(\mathcal{N}(i)\) denotes neighboring Gaussians in the partition. This formulation allows us to decouple optimization without a central server.

Why does it matter?

Previous large-scale SfM methods (like GraphSfM) required explicit stitching. With 3DGS, we can average raw Gaussians directly, which is differentiable and preserves details. The following video demonstrates the reconstruction of a 5km urban scene:

Mathematical foundation

The consensus algorithm builds on the formulation:

\[ \min_{\{\mathbf{G}_i\}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{L}_i(\mathbf{G}_i) + \lambda \sum_{(i,j)\in \mathcal{E}} \|\mathbf{G}_i - \mathbf{G}_j\|^2 \]

This encourages neighboring Gaussians to agree while preserving local details. The optimization alternates between local gradient steps and consensus averaging.

"Distributed 3DGS is not just about scale, it's about robustness to dynamic changes."

Results and discussion

We evaluated DOGS on several large-scale datasets:

  • Urban scenes (10k+ images): 40% faster reconstruction than sequential methods
  • Object-centric (CO3D): comparable quality to centralized 3DGS
  • Aerial mapping: seamless stitching across 20km² area

For more details, check our project page or paper.
